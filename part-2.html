01 · The design at a glance

A loop that keeps humans in charge.

An AI suggestion stays tentative until checked. An accepted suggestion becomes an open follow-up—not a confirmed decision or completed work.

Context loopOpen Mermaid source ↗
02 · Memory design

Remember the decision, not just the alert.

The brief should preserve enough context to avoid re-investigating the same question while keeping uncertainty explicit.

01

Evidence ledger

Keep the source link, observation date, coverage, and last successful check. A failed refresh is visible; it never becomes “no change.”

02

Decision record

Capture confirmed decisions, their source, the responsible owner, and the checkpoint that makes the decision actionable.

03

Follow-up state

Track accepted, dismissed-with-reason, deferred, and due items. Read status alone never closes the action.

04

Source health

Show what was checked, what was unavailable, and where coverage is incomplete so missing context cannot masquerade as stability.

✦
What stays human

Priority, ownership, scope, outreach, escalation, and release decisions stay with the responsible people. The tool reads and drafts; the TPM verifies and follows through using normal team channels.

03 · Acceptance checks

Test the failure modes before expanding.

These are proposed checks for the first-month trial—not measured results. The trial continues only if it earns trust alongside manual triage.

✓

Resolved blocker, release still on hold

Keep the release hold visible when the named consensus blocker is resolved but other release criteria remain open.

✓

Deliberately paused work

Respect a confirmed pause and keep it quiet until the agreed checkpoint or a new source change.

✓

Failed source refresh

Report “status not refreshed” with the failed source and last known coverage rather than infer that nothing changed.

✓

Conflicting evidence

Surface the disagreement and its sources for review; never silently choose a winner or turn a suggestion into a fact.

Continue only if
The trial reduces daily review time, keeps confirmed pauses quiet, and surfaces the important changes found in manual checks.

WIP dashboards and broader reporting can wait until the daily brief proves useful.

04 · First-month plan

Start small. Learn where the context breaks.

One release and NU7 are enough to test the loop. Coverage grows only after the review path and recovery behavior are credible.

WEEK 01

Agree the frame

Sources, commitments, pause rules, and examples to test.

WEEK 02

Trial repository collection

Run a private brief alongside manual triage.

WEEK 03

Add selected conversations

Test forum/chat sources, pauses, conflict, and missed-run recovery.

WEEK 04

Assess and adjust

Measure usefulness and maintenance effort before expanding coverage.

05 · Example morning brief

Three follow-ups for review.

Illustrative output from the Part 1 snapshot. Proposed actions remain drafts until reviewed and sent.

01

Zebra release: confirm remaining holds

Why it matters: a failed wallet test and advisory semver check need a release decision.

Next: confirm criteria, owner, and decision date.

02

NU7: confirm external contacts and dates

Why it matters: branch IDs, a librustzcash release, and activation heights sit across the delivery path.

Next: confirm needed-by dates and what happens if one slips.

03

Key-conversion fix: confirm reviewer pickup

Why it matters: impact assessment and independent review are needed before deciding urgency and release path.

Next: identify affected versions, callers, and a reviewer.

Deliberately not chasing: items without a decision, owner, or new evidence stay available for review rather than crowding out the next three actions.
